## Discussion Summary

### Theory of change vs "theory of build" (Durgadas, central thesis)
Durgadas pressed — gently but with discipline, as follow-up from prior strategy workshops — that the project began without a "specific, actionable, time-bound, measurable" underlying problem, risking becoming "a thing in search of a problem" where the team "shoehorns problems in to fit the thing you've already built." He named this a "**theory of build over theory of change**" failure mode (from his proof-of-coordination research) — endemic across Web3, a "frame two failure mode" (all-you-have-to-do-is-build). Crucially he is *not* against theories of build: "you can have multiple theories of build inside of your theory of change… but a theory of build alone yields no strategy."

### What problem the toolkit solves (Matty + Heenal)
Matty's two-problem answer: (1) a cohesive resource for Web3-regen local nodes/chapters to run their orgs (vs scattered stuff + ad-hoc calls); (2) a discoverable **map** so newcomers find relevant tools/protocols/orgs by interest instead of "wandering the jungle." Durgadas distilled: "so basically, onboarding." Heenal gave the origin: Green Pill + ReFi DAO each started toolkit efforts ~18 months ago to curate resources for distinct audiences (Web3-curious newcomers; social-impact people adopting Web3; Web3-natives new to coordination/impact); it "meandered" into something bigger about knowledge categorization and paths-through-knowledge.

### Koi's research-framework analogy + civilizational frame
Koi (agroecology background) gave an extended analogy: a field with no "best practices" because the only established practices were the extractive ones being fought — researchers split between re-proving the old paradigm wrong (complicated domain) and running small experiments (complex domain, unknown outcome). Mapped onto governance: the old paradigm treats onboarding as a cost to minimize (IPO/ICO heritage); the regenerative frame treats it as continuous coordination. He framed the work as addressing a **civilizational problem** (Greenpill's "third attractor") needing a collective "immune system"/sensor for the ecosystem (e.g. shared registry of scams/bad actors).

### Knowledge commons, ontology & Geo Protocol (Rather)
Rather reported deploying a **forkable metadata ontology to Geo Protocol** — regen-relevant content types/relations that didn't previously exist in the knowledge graph (labels, not strict taxonomy; multiple types per entity). He defended knowledge-commons work as legitimate per se (Ostrom-grounded): real implicit problems (agreeing schemas/data shapes, shared language) justify it even absent a single headline problem. Durgadas pushed back on the Ostrom invocation — Ostrom's commons is "extremely specific, technical, structural, process-oriented," not generic "commoning" — arguing the group is "starting from the middle" (neither problem-first nor solution-first), and modeling disconfirmation as a live discipline.

### Framework vs instance ("toolkit or knowledge?")
Koi asked directly: **"Are we building the toolkit or are we building the knowledge? They are not the same thing"** — proposing the toolkit be treated as *part of* a larger "Regen knowledge" body. Matty's counter: build a "very broad thing… an encyclopedia/library of everything," then generate **custom tracks/flows** per individual or org need. *(This is the same framework-vs-instance distinction that the 2026-06-15 Matty+Luiz session resolved toward a formal split.)*

### Frame language / metrics (brief)
Durgadas noted he built the frame-language tool for semantic interpolation across disjoint vocabularies; Koi proposed integrating the **Frame Language Analyzer** to generate customized tradeoff data with context. On metrics: Koi proposed measuring by goal (knowledge-sharing → citation/reuse of posted research; onboarding → time-to-wallet, time-to-first-transaction, time-to-governance, downstream tool usage). Afo pushed that "get a wallet, do a transaction" goals feel dated as the space matures.
