# Setting Up Your First Wallet

A grant comes through for your community garden, and someone says they'll send the funds "to your wallet." You nod along, quietly panicking, because you've never set one up and you've heard the horror stories about people losing everything. Take a breath. Setting up a wallet is closer to planting a seed tray than defusing a bomb, and by the end of this you'll have done it without handing your money to a stranger.

## What a Wallet Actually Is

A **crypto wallet** is an app (or small device) that lets you receive, hold, and send digital money and other tokens. It does not "contain" your coins the way a leather wallet holds cash. It holds the *keys* that prove the coins are yours, a bit like the deed to a plot of land rather than the soil itself.

The important shift: with a traditional bank, the bank holds your money and can freeze, reverse, or recover it. With a **self-custody wallet** (also called non-custodial, meaning *you* hold the keys, not a company), you are the only one in charge. That's the freedom and the responsibility. You're tending your own patch, not renting space in someone else's field.

## Hot Wallets and Cold Wallets

There are two broad types, and the difference is simply whether they touch the internet.

**Hot wallets** are apps connected to the internet, on your phone or in your browser. MetaMask, Rainbow, and Coinbase Wallet are all hot wallets, all self-custody (you hold the keys), and all free. They're like the herbs on your windowsill: easy to reach, perfect for everyday use and small amounts.

**Cold wallets** are small physical devices that keep your keys offline, only connecting when you choose to. Ledger and Trezor are the two best-known makers. They're like a seed vault with a locked door: more effort to open, far harder for anyone to reach. They're worth buying once you're holding a meaningful amount you don't touch often.

For your very first wallet, start hot. A few potted herbs before the whole food forest. This guide uses **MetaMask**, one of the most widely used self-custody wallets, because nearly every Web3 project supports it.

## Your Secret Recovery Phrase: Guard This With Your Life

When you create a wallet, you'll be given a list of 12 random words. MetaMask calls this your **Secret Recovery Phrase** (you'll also hear "seed phrase"). These words *are* your wallet. Anyone who has them controls your money, and if you lose them, no one on earth can get your money back. There is no "forgot password" link and no support line that can help.

Think of it as the living genetic code of a rare heirloom seed. Copy it down faithfully and you can always regrow your garden on a new device. Lose it and the variety is gone for good.

A few rules that will save you real money:

- **Write the 12 words on paper, by hand.** Not a screenshot, not a note on your phone, not a password manager, not a cloud document. Just paper.
- **Make two copies and store them in two separate safe places.** A fire or a flood shouldn't be able to wipe out both.
- **Never type your recovery phrase into a website or app, ever, for any reason.** A real app never asks for it during normal use. Anyone who asks is robbing you. This single habit prevents the most common way beginners get drained.

For long-term storage, some people stamp the words into steel so fire and water can't destroy them, but a couple of paper copies are a fine start.

## Your Wallet Address: Safe to Share

Your **wallet address** is a long string starting with `0x`, like `0x7a25...c4f9`. This is the part you *do* share. It's how people send funds to you, the way you'd give someone the address of the garden so they can drop off compost.

The mental model that keeps people safe: your address is like your email address (share it freely), and your recovery phrase is like your email password (never share it). They are not the same thing, and nothing that asks for your "address" needs your phrase.

## Setting Up MetaMask, Step by Step

Set aside ten quiet minutes where no one's rushing you. You'll do this once.

1. **Go to the real site.** Type `metamask.io` into your browser yourself, or use the app store on your phone. Never install from a link in an email, ad, or DM. Fake MetaMask sites are a known trap, so bookmark the real one now.
2. **Install it.** On a computer, add the browser extension (Chrome, Brave, Firefox, or Edge). On a phone, download the official MetaMask app.
3. **Choose "Create a new wallet."** You'll be asked to agree to the terms first.
4. **Pick the recovery-phrase option.** MetaMask now offers two paths: a Secret Recovery Phrase, or a "social login" tied to a Google or Apple account. For learning how self-custody actually works, choose the **Secret Recovery Phrase** option. It keeps you fully in control.
5. **Create a password.** This unlocks the app on this one device only; it is not your recovery phrase and can't restore your wallet elsewhere. Make it long and unique.
6. **Reveal and write down your 12 words.** Copy them exactly, in order, on paper. Double-check the spelling.
7. **Confirm the phrase.** MetaMask asks you to re-select the words in order to prove you really saved them. This is your checkpoint, so take it seriously.
8. **You're in.** Your wallet opens and shows your `0x` address, ready to receive funds.

That's it. You now hold your own keys.

## A Tiny Bit of "Gas" to Get Moving

To *send* anything (not to receive, which is free), you need a small amount of cryptocurrency to pay the network fee. This fee is called **gas**: think of it as the postage stamp on a letter. The network charges it to process and secure your transaction.

Here's the good news that older guides miss. On Ethereum's main network, fees rise and fall and can sometimes sting. But most regen and community projects now run on **layer 2 networks** (faster, cheaper add-on networks that settle back to Ethereum), such as **Base**, **Optimism**, and **Arbitrum**. On these, a transaction typically costs a fraction of a cent. The "expensive gas" fear you may have heard is mostly about the old main network, not where you'll actually be working.

You don't need to buy much. A couple of dollars of the right token covers a lot of small transactions. A teammate can send you a little, or you can buy a small amount directly in the wallet when you're ready.

## Building Your Garden Fence: Staying Safe

A few simple habits protect you from nearly everything beginners worry about:

- **Bookmark `metamask.io`** and only ever go there directly. Most "MetaMask support" messages are scams; real support never DMs you first.
- **Turn on phishing protection** in MetaMask's settings, which warns you about known scam sites.
- **Start with tiny amounts** while you learn. A test transaction of pennies teaches you the flow with nothing at stake.
- **Slow down when something feels urgent.** "Verify your wallet now or lose access" is always a lie. Pressure is the scammer's main tool.

You don't have to be technical to be safe here. You have to be a little stubborn about two things: never sharing your recovery phrase, and never trusting urgency.

## Try This

> **Start here:** Open the real `metamask.io` in your browser today and read the homepage. Don't install yet. Just confirm the address, bookmark it, and get comfortable with what the genuine site looks like so a fake one can't fool you later.

> **Go deeper:** Install MetaMask and create a wallet following the steps above. Write your 12 words on two pieces of paper and store them in two different places. Then copy your `0x` address and ask a trusted friend to send you a tiny test amount on a layer 2 like Base.

> **Stretch:** Once your hot wallet feels routine and you're holding more than pocket change, research a hardware wallet (Ledger or Trezor), set it up, and move the bulk of your funds to it. Keep your hot wallet for day-to-day use and your cold wallet as your seed vault.
