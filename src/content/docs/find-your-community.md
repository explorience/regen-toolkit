# Find Your Community

You have heard that people are using crypto to fund tree-planting, soil restoration, and neighbourhood projects, and a small part of you is curious. But every time you go looking, you hit a wall of jargon, hype, and stories about people losing their savings to scammers.

That wariness is healthy. The good news: there are real, grounded communities full of people who care about the same things you do, and you can find them without spending money, sharing anything sensitive, or pretending to understand things you do not. This is a guide to finding your people, the same way you would find a good seed-swap or a trustworthy gardening group, just in an unfamiliar field.

## Start with the field you already know

Think of this space the way you think of a regional growers' network. There is no single "crypto community." There are many overlapping circles, each with its own focus and culture, loosely connected like a mycelial network under a forest.

The corner you want is called **ReFi**, short for **regenerative finance**: tools and projects built to create economies that restore ecosystems and communities rather than deplete them. Ethereum's own plain-language explainer describes ReFi as systems that "create value for participants while simultaneously benefiting ecosystems and communities" — closer to compost than to a casino.

You do not need to understand the underlying technology to join the conversation. You need to find the people, listen for a while, and decide if they share your values. That is a skill you already have.

## Two real front doors

You want named, established places run by people who put their work in public, not a random group someone DMs you about. Two networks are good first doors, and both are free to walk through.

**ReFi DAO** calls itself "a network society to regenerate the Earth." (A **DAO**, or decentralised autonomous organisation, is just a group that coordinates and makes decisions in the open online — think of it as a co-op with a shared online noticeboard instead of a back office.) Its most useful feature for you is the **Local Nodes** directory at refidao.com/local-nodes: a sortable list of place-based ReFi communities around the world. You can sort by location to see whether anything exists near you, and there are clear "Join the Network" and "Start a Local Node" paths if you want to plant one yourself.

**Greenpill Network** is the second door. It runs local **chapters** — neighbourhood groups that meet, learn, and fund small local projects together. As of mid-2026 its site lists twelve active chapters across four regions, including Cape Town, Nairobi, Taipei, Rio de Janeiro, New York City, and London, Ontario, Canada. You can see them all on the chapter map at greenpill.network.

Both networks are place-based on purpose. The whole point is to connect online tools to on-the-ground work, which is exactly the bridge you are trying to cross.

## Listen before you commit

The biggest fear here is usually not money — it is looking foolish, or pouring hours into hype that goes nowhere. So treat your first weeks as eavesdropping, not joining.

Greenpill is built for exactly this. It offers four entry points at increasing levels of commitment, and you are encouraged to start at the shallow end:

1. **Subscribe to the field notes** — a monthly email of what chapters are actually doing. Zero commitment, no account, just reading.
2. **Join the public Telegram chat** — Telegram is a free messaging app, like a group text. Drop in and read. You are allowed to lurk.
3. **Take the Regen Assessment** — a short questionnaire about your interests, used to point you toward a chapter or working group.
4. **Book a steward call** — a real conversation, only once you actually want one.

This is the same instinct you would use walking into a new community garden: stand at the edge, watch how people treat each other, see whether the work is real, and only pick up a trowel when you are ready. A healthy community will be glad to have you listen first.

If you prefer a slower, asynchronous pace, you can also follow the **Greenpill** podcast, hosted by Kevin Owocki (a co-founder of the grant-funding platform Gitcoin), which has run for hundreds of episodes interviewing people doing this work. It is a low-pressure way to learn the language and the names before you ever say a word.

## How to tell a real community from a trap

This is the part worth slowing down for, because a few simple rules will keep you safe from almost everything.

**No one in a real community will ever ask for your seed phrase.** A **seed phrase** (also called a recovery phrase) is the list of words that unlocks a crypto wallet — it is the master key to your money. Security guidance from wallet makers and researchers like Kaspersky is blunt and unanimous: no legitimate person, support team, or moderator will ever ask for it, on any platform, for any reason. Anyone who does is robbing you. Treat that request the way you would treat a "bank official" demanding your PIN by phone: it is the scam, every time.

**Real teams do not slide into your DMs first.** A near-universal red flag, repeated across security write-ups from Ledger and others, is the unsolicited direct message offering "support," a "giveaway," or a "verification" link. Legitimate communities answer questions in public channels. If a stranger DMs you a link or an urgent opportunity, assume it is bait.

**Always reach a community through its own official site.** Scammers clone Discord servers, websites, and social accounts. The safe habit is to type the known address yourself (greenpill.network, refidao.com) and follow the links from there, rather than clicking a link someone hands you. Bookmark the real ones once you find them.

A few more grounding rules:

- **You should never need to send money first.** Joining a community, reading, and showing up to a call cost nothing. "Send funds to receive funds" is always a scam.
- **Big numbers are not proof.** A server with thousands of members can still be hollow or hostile. Judge by what people actually do and how they treat newcomers, not by the headcount.
- **Slow is safe.** Urgency ("act now, spots are limited") is a manipulation tactic. Real regenerative work moves at the pace of trust, like building soil.

If a place fails these tests, leave. There are plenty that pass.

## Try This

> **Start here:** Today, open greenpill.network and refidao.com/local-nodes (type the addresses yourself). Sort the ReFi DAO Local Nodes directory by location to see if anything exists near you, and read three chapter descriptions on Greenpill. Notice how the real work is described. Cost: zero. Risk: zero.

> **Go deeper:** Subscribe to Greenpill's monthly field notes and join its public Telegram chat. Lurk for two weeks. Read, do not post. Write down three things real people are actually building and one question you would want to ask. You are gathering intelligence, not committing to anything.

> **Stretch:** If a nearby Local Node or chapter exists, introduce yourself in the public channel and attend one online or in-person meeting as a guest. If none exists near you, read ReFi DAO's "Start a Local Node" path and sketch what a node rooted in your existing community garden, co-op, or organising network could look like. You already know how to gather people — this is the same craft on new soil.
