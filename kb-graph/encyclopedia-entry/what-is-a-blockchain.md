# What Is a Blockchain?

Plain-language, no-hype introduction to blockchains via a "village notebook" analogy: many computers (nodes) keep identical copies of a record, batched into blocks that hash-chain to make history tamper-evident ("immutable"), removing the need to trust any single keeper ("trustless") and solving double-spending without a referee. Covers real regenerative/community examples (Grassroots Economics' Sarafu Network, Regen Network), an enterprise comparison (Walmart on Hyperledger Fabric), and an honest costs section (energy use differences between proof-of-work and proof-of-stake, permanence cutting both ways, and that blockchains record agreement but don't create it).
