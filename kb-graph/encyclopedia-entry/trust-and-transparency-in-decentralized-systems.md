# Trust and Transparency in Decentralized Systems

Argues decentralization does not eliminate trust but relocates it — from institutions (banks, platforms) to open-source code, consensus mechanisms, and public verifiability. Explains how blockchain properties (immutability, transparency, cryptographic security) enable this, walks through Proof of Work vs Proof of Stake as the "trust engine," then names a "transparency paradox": the same public visibility that enables accountability (detecting insider trading, concentrated ownership) also creates attack surfaces (sophisticated actors planning attacks on visible positions). Closes on decentralized identity (DIDs) as returning ownership of one's digital self.
